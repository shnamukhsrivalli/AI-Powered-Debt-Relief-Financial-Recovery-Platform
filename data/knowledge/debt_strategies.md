# Debt Repayment Strategies: Avalanche vs. Snowball

When managing multiple outstanding debts, choosing a structured repayment strategy is critical to ensure success and maintain focus. Two of the most widely recommended strategies are the **Debt Avalanche** and **Debt Snowball** methods.

## The Debt Avalanche Method (APR Prioritization)
The Debt Avalanche strategy is mathematically optimal. It prioritizes accounts based on interest rate (APR):
1. List all active debts.
2. Sort them from highest interest rate to lowest interest rate.
3. Make minimum required payments on all accounts.
4. Direct all extra surplus cash to the account with the highest interest rate.
5. Once the highest-rate account is paid off, roll its entire payment (minimum + extra) into the next highest-rate account.
**Benefit**: Minimizes the total interest paid over time and reduces overall payoff duration.

## The Debt Snowball Method (Balance Prioritization)
The Debt Snowball strategy prioritizes accounts based on balance size:
1. List all active debts.
2. Sort them from smallest outstanding balance to largest outstanding balance.
3. Make minimum required payments on all accounts.
4. Direct all extra surplus cash to the account with the smallest balance.
5. Once the smallest account is paid off, roll its entire payment into the next smallest account.
**Benefit**: Provides quick psychological wins and reduces the number of open accounts rapidly, which builds emotional momentum.

## Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio
The Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio is calculated as:
`DTI = (Total Monthly Debt Payments / Gross Monthly Income) * 100`
It is a primary indicator of debt risk used by financial lenders:
- **Under 20%**: Excellent, low risk.
- **20% to 36%**: Healthy, manageable.
- **36% to 50%**: Elevated risk; lenders look closely at additional assets.
- **Above 50%**: Critical risk; indicates severe debt strain.
